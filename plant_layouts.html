Plant layouts — animated comparison

Four classic manufacturing layouts: jobshop, flow line, project shop, and group technology.

Job shop (process layout)

Machines of the same type are grouped together. Each job has its own routing sequence, visiting only the departments it needs.

Best for
Low-volume, high-variety work
Flow pattern
Jumbled, criss-cross routes
Labour
Skilled, flexible operators
Real examples
Machine shops, hospitals, restaurants
High flexibility Fault-tolerant Complex scheduling High WIP Long throughput time
Flow line (product layout)

Machines are arranged in the order of operations. All products move in one continuous, sequential flow from raw material to finished good.

Best for
High-volume, standardised products
Flow pattern
Single straight line, one-way
Labour
Specialised, repetitive tasks
Real examples
Car assembly, bottling, PCB lines
High throughput Low WIP Simple control Low flexibility Breakdown halts line High capital cost
Project shop (fixed-position layout)

The product is too large, heavy, or delicate to move. Instead, everything needed to build it — workers, materials, tools — is brought to a single stationary site.

Best for
One-off, large-scale products
Flow pattern
Resources converge inward
Labour
Multi-disciplinary teams
Real examples
Shipyards, construction, aircraft
Handles any size Product never moves Complex logistics High coordination Very high unit cost Slow output rate
Group technology (cellular manufacturing)

A hybrid approach. Parts are classified into families based on similar shape or processing, and each family gets its own self-contained cell. Within a cell, a part may move back and forth between machines as operations require.

Best for
Medium-volume, medium-variety
Flow pattern
Bidirectional inside cell
Labour
Multi-skilled cell operators
Real examples
Aerospace, electronics, automotive parts
Low WIP vs job shop Short routes Medium flexibility Needs family analysis Upfront reorganisation
