Theory: the factor rating method

Sⱼ = Σᵢ wᵢ · scoreᵢⱼ / Σᵢ wᵢ

  1. List the factors that matter (labor, logistics, taxes, quality of life…).
  2. Weight each factor by importance.
  3. Score every site on every factor on a common scale.
  4. The highest weighted total wins — but always test sensitivity: if a small weight change flips the winner, the decision needs more analysis, not more arithmetic.

Locational break-even — the best site depends on volume

Each site has a fixed cost (land, building, taxes) and a variable cost per unit (labor, transport). Total cost TC = FC + VC·Q is a straight line — and the cheapest site changes as volume grows. Drag the volume slider along the chart.

Theory: crossover points

Two sites tie where their cost lines cross: Q* = (FC₂ − FC₁)/(VC₁ − VC₂).

Low-fixed/high-variable sites win at small volumes; high-fixed/low-variable sites win at large volumes. The decision is only as good as the volume forecast — try moving the slider across a crossover and imagine the forecast being wrong by that much.

Theory: the transportation model

Minimize Σᵢⱼ cᵢⱼ·xᵢⱼ subject to shipping exactly each plant's supply and meeting each market's demand.

  • Northwest Corner ignores costs entirely — it just produces a feasible start.
  • Least Cost greedily fills the cheapest available cell.
  • VAM allocates where the penalty for not using the cheapest route is largest — usually the best start.

These are starting solutions; full optimality is then verified with MODI / stepping-stone (next course level — or a solver). If your manual allocation beats VAM, take a screenshot.
